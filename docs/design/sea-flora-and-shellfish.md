# Sea flora and shellfish

Status: idea
Scope: both (waterlogged blocks and their meshing, underwater placement, picking and regrowth, the ingredients they give)

Sister to the fish idea (#1523). The fish idea fills the water with things that swim; this one covers the sea floor with things that grow and cling: kelp, seagrass, sea urchins, oysters and scallops. Kelp becomes nori for maki, and the shellfish become their own sushi. They are all "flora" here: blocks placed when the world is generated, picked by hand and grown back, not entities.

biome-flora moved underwater flora out of its own phases and asked for this doc (decided in #1466): it needs a block that also draws as the water around it. That block type is the core of this idea.

There is no underwater flora in Beta, so this is Mince Raft's own design. Nothing here needs SPEC research. Every number lives in data and is tuned for feel.

---

## 1. Design rules

1. Sea flora lives only in water and only where the habitat suits it. Cold kelp forests and warm urchin reefs should make the route from cold to warm feel different, as fish do.
2. A sea flora block is also water. Swimming through kelp is swimming; the player never stands on, bumps into or gets stuck in sea flora.
3. Picking follows the existing picking rules (picking-plants): the item goes to the hands, one item per trip.
4. The sea is a daily pantry, like the fish stock: picked flora comes back each morning.
5. Placement is deterministic: a sea flora block depends only on the seed, the layout and its world coordinate, never on the region it is generated in.

---

## 2. Waterlogged blocks

A new block property, `waterlogged = true` in `data/blocks.toml`.

- **Physics.** A waterlogged cell counts as water for every water rule: body-in-water detection, water movement, swim up, climbing out and falling into water (basic-gameplay 4.3, 4.6). It has no collision box.
- **Meshing.** The mesher draws a waterlogged cell as a water cell (same faces, tint and animation as `water`, including the surface quad when it is the top water cell) plus the plant's own geometry inside it. Neighbouring water treats it as water, so no faces appear between them. This applies to both meshers: the viewer and client mesher today and the Rust mesher being built in viewer-rendering Phase E.
- **Targeting.** Every waterlogged cell has a selection box (the plant's own, smaller than the cell where the plant is small).
  - When the eye is in water, the ray passes through plain water cells and stops on sea flora, blocks and entities.
  - When the eye is above water, the ray still stops on the water surface, as decided in #174, so fishing and other water targets are unchanged. A waterlogged cell at the surface (the top of a kelp column) is targeted as the plant, not as water.
- **Shapes.** `cross` (seagrass), `column` (kelp: a stack of cross cells) and `cluster` (shellfish: small boxes on the bottom face of the cell).

> **Reconciler:** The Rust mesher in `crates/mesh` is built (Phase E is `done`), not "being built"; see notes 2 to 4.

---

## 3. What grows where

| Flora | Shape | Habitats | Bottom | Depth | Gives |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| kelp | column, 3 to 12 cells tall, top at least 1 below the surface or touching it | `cold_open`, `temperate_open`, `rocky_shore` | gravel, stone, sand | 4 to 16 | `kelp` |
| seagrass | cross | `warm_shallows`, `temperate_open` | sand | 1 to 6 | nothing (cover only) |
| sea urchin | cluster | `rocky_shore`, `warm_shallows` | stone, gravel | 2 to 8 | `sea_urchin` |
| oyster | cluster | `rocky_shore`, `temperate_open` near shore | gravel, stone | 1 to 5 | `oyster` |
| scallop | cluster | `cold_open`, `temperate_open` | sand, gravel | 3 to 12 | `scallop` |

All of this is data in a new `data/sea_flora.toml`, in the style of `data/flora.toml`: block, shape, habitats, bottom blocks, depth range, cluster scale, salt and a chance per eligible column. Clusters use patch noise so kelp forms forests and oysters form beds, not an even sprinkle.

Kelp columns grow only up from a bottom block. Shellfish sit on the bottom block, one per cell. Nothing grows in `frozen` or `deep_water` columns, or in the route channel (so the raft never drags through a kelp forest), the spawn water radius or under the raft.

> **Reconciler:** `rocky_shore` is defined as depth 8 or less, so a kelp depth of 4 to 16 only reaches 4 to 8 there; `warm_shallows` is depth 6 or less on sand only, so urchins (stone, gravel) cannot occur there as `habitats.toml` stands (the habitat needs a sand seabed). "Near shore" for oysters needs a `shore_distance` limit in data. See open question 8.

---

## 4. Placement

- A sea flora pass in `crates/layout`, after decoration and after the habitat map, keyed on habitat, bottom block and depth. `crates/terrain` does not learn about it, and every terrain block is unchanged; only water cells become waterlogged flora.
- The pass decides each column from hashes of the seed and the world coordinate, plus the column's habitat. Since the habitat map already agrees across region borders, the flora does too.
- Validation (world-layout 6.1) gains: no sea flora in the channel's navigable set, the spawn water radius or the raft footprint.
- The viewer's habitat mode gains a sea flora overlay, so seeds can be judged by eye.

---

## 5. Picking and regrowth

- **Pick.** Secondary with empty hands or any tool (matching the existing `pick` rules for crops and flowers) on a sea flora block, within block reach, picks it. Shellfish and seagrass are removed. For kelp, picking any cell takes that cell and every kelp cell above it as one `kelp` item, and the stub below stays. Uses the world block-edit layer (#512): the cell becomes plain water.
- Seagrass gives no item. Picking it just clears it, like trimming.
- **Regrowth.** At the start of each day, the edits that removed sea flora are cleared, so every picked kelp, urchin, oyster and scallop is back in the morning. This is the sea's daily stock, like the fish stock. Other block edits are unaffected.
- From the raft, the top of a kelp column at the surface is in reach; shellfish need a dive.
- Add the rules to `data/interactions.toml` (`target = "block:sea_flora"`, `action = "pick"`), with `held = "empty"` and `held = "any_tool"` rows as for flowers, and `[[pickable]]` entries in `data/items.toml` for each block and item.

> **Reconciler:** Seagrass has no item, but `[[pickable]]` pairs a block with an item (`data/items.toml`); seagrass needs either a pickable with no item or a separate "clear" action. Kelp picked as "that cell and every cell above" must not leave a kelp stub whose top is now far below the surface that still counts as a column; it is fine, since the stub is just a shorter column. "At the start of each day": the day length is in `data/phases.toml`; say whether a day starts at tick 0 of each gathering phase (the first phase) or at every service-to-gathering change.
